Model

A sail of size D and areal density σ carries a payload m₀ and is pushed by a laser array of size d, power P and wavelength λ. Formulas follow the UCSB 1-D laser propulsion calculators and Lubin (2016).

m = σ ξ D² + m₀   (ξ = 1 square, π/4 circular)     η = 2R + (1−R)A     a₀ = η P / (m c)     L₀ = d D / (2 λ α)

Beam capture. The diffraction-limited spot grows linearly with distance. It is smaller than the sail until L₀, and beyond that only a fraction f = (L₀/x)² of the power hits the sail. α = 1.22 for a circular array and 1 for a square one, times a beam-quality factor ≥ 1.

Classical:   dv/dt = a₀ f(x)      Relativistic:   c·d(γβ)/dt = a₀ f(x) · (1−β)/(1+β)

In the sail's frame the received power is Doppler-reduced by (1−β)/(1+β): photon energy and photon arrival rate each drop by √((1−β)/(1+β)). With the work integral W(x) = ∫ f dx (= x inside L₀, 2L₀ − L₀²/x beyond), both models have closed-form solutions:

Classical: v = √(2 a₀ W)  ⇒  v∞ = √2 · v(L₀)      Relativistic: (𝒟−1)²(𝒟+2) = 6 a₀ W / c²,  𝒟 = √((1+β)/(1−β))

Both corrections depend on a single number, β₀ = √(2 a₀ L₀)/c, the classical speed at L₀. Earth time comes from t = ∫ dx/v and ship time from τ = ∫ dt/γ. Inside L₀ these are closed form: t = (c/a₀)(𝒟³+3𝒟−4)/6 and τ = (c/a₀)(𝒟²−1)/2. Beyond L₀ they are computed with Gauss–Legendre quadrature in ln x.

Assumptions

  • One-dimensional motion, normal incidence, flat rigid sail riding the beam stably (no tilt, spin or deformation).
  • Constant laser power. Laser-on time is set by the sail's distance (on until x_stop), so the light-travel delay after switch-off is not modelled.
  • Idealized diffraction: full capture inside L₀, (L₀/x)² beyond. No atmosphere, pointing jitter or side-lobes.
  • Opaque sail by default: light that isn't reflected is absorbed (A = 1), then re-radiated isotropically in the sail frame, so the rest mass is constant. Thermal limits are reported (peak equilibrium temperature) but not enforced.
  • No gravity (Earth, Sun), no interstellar drag or dust, no deceleration at the target (flyby).

Validation

  • Classical outputs reproduce the UCSB classical calculator to machine precision: sail mass, L₀, acceleration, time to L₀, speed at L₀, limiting speed, travel time and temperature.
  • Relativistic speeds match the UCSB relativistic calculator's lookup tables to their tabulated precision. Its limiting-speed formula is algebraically identical to the cubic above.
  • The closed forms agree with direct numerical integration (SciPy solve_ivp, DOP853, rtol 10⁻¹¹) to about 10⁻¹¹. This JavaScript engine agrees with the Python reference to better than 10⁻¹⁰.
  • One discrepancy: the relativistic calculator's time to L₀ expression equals the integrated value multiplied by βclassical/βrelativistic. That makes it about 12 % too long for the default inputs (180 s vs 161 s).
